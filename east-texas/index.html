Sleet is mixing in near Dallas. A few pellets could reach us this afternoon, then freezing mornings on Thanksgiving and Friday.

A light band of rain is moving east from the Metroplex, and spotters from Fort Worth to Waco to Ennis have reported sleet mixed in this morning, even with temperatures in the mid 40s. The air near the ground is very dry, so falling precipitation cools itself as it evaporates and some of it reaches the ground as sleet or soft ice pellets. If the band holds together it reaches the Tyler area around 3 to 5 PM. Most models weaken it before it gets here, so the chance of any precipitation is low, but a brief burst could include a few pellets. Temperatures stay well above freezing, so there is no ice on roads. Clearing behind it sets up freezing mornings Thursday and Friday.

This afternoon

Why sleet can fall at 45 degrees

This morning's weather balloon at Fort Worth found the air freezing about a mile up, with very dry air below it. Snow falling from that cold layer starts to melt, but evaporation into the dry air cools it back down, so some of it lands as sleet or soft graupel pellets. That is exactly what spotters reported from Fort Worth to Ennis.

The Shreveport balloon was warmer and moister aloft this morning, but cooler air moves in this evening as the disturbance passes. The best odds of a few pellets are west of Tyler and during any heavier burst. Precipitation will be light and brief, and ground temperatures stay in the 40s, so there is no travel impact.

Thanksgiving week

Two cold mornings, then a warm weekend

NWS Shreveport says a quick upper-level disturbance passes tonight with clouds and a slight chance of light rain, mainly south and east of Tyler. Behind it, lows fall near or below freezing Thursday and Friday mornings, from the mid 20s to mid 30s across the region.

Thanksgiving Day looks sunny with highs in the mid 50s. Highs return to the upper 60s and low 70s this weekend, with a small chance of showers Saturday night into Sunday. NWS notes the drought continues with no meaningful rain in sight.

Looking ahead

Warmer than normal into early December

The Climate Prediction Center favors above-normal temperatures and above-normal precipitation for Texas from Nov 29 to Dec 7, with about average confidence. Most European and GFS ensemble members show at least half an inch of rain for the area sometime Dec 1–8, so the first real rain chance in a while may come early next week.

El Niño remains strong to very strong. It tends to favor an active southern storm track this winter, though NWS Fort Worth notes a strongly negative Pacific pattern may keep it drier than usual.
