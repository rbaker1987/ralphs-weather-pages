A quick burst of mountain snow today, then the coldest night of the season.

A Pacific cold front is crossing the mountains, with snow showers into the afternoon. Most areas get under 2 inches, and the highest southwest-facing slopes of the Sangres could see up to 4 or 5 inches. By mid-morning, backyard stations in the mountain towns read mostly in the mid 30s to low 40s and falling. Tonight clears and turns very cold, with single digits in high valleys like Angel Fire and Red River.

Snow and cold

Quick snow today, coldest night tonight

NWS Albuquerque says today's front brings a short burst of snow to the Jemez, Sangre de Cristo and Sandia/Manzano mountains, generally under 2 inches. Southwest-facing Sangre slopes above 10,000 ft could pick up as much as 5 inches.

Skies clear tonight and winds ease, so cold air settles into the valleys. NWS forecasts about 4 to 11 degrees at Angel Fire and near 11 at Red River, the coldest of the season so far. Protect outdoor pipes and pets.

Roads & fire

Mountain roads open, watch for slick spots

As of late morning, NMRoads lists no weather problems on NM-518, US-64, NM-434, NM-38, NM-75, NM-150, NM-68, US-285/84 or NM-475. Expect road work on NM-434 south of Black Lake (MM 21–24).

Snow showers this afternoon can make passes and shaded canyons slick quickly, and tonight's cold will refreeze anything wet. Monday night's system may do the same.

NWS expects no critical fire weather for the next seven days.

Looking ahead

Dry around Thanksgiving, then maybe more active

NWS Albuquerque expects a mostly dry week after Monday, with temperatures near to slightly below normal. Longer-range guidance points toward wetter and colder weather in early December, and OpenSnow is watching Nov 30 to Dec 2.

El Niño is strong and still strengthening. It usually sends more storms across the southern Rockies, which improves the odds for a snowier season here, though it does not guarantee one.
